Research

Constraint-aware graph learning, across scales

Most "physics-informed" neural networks encode domain knowledge as a soft penalty in the loss function — a preference the model can still violate if it's undertrained or pushed off-distribution. My work takes a different approach: encode the constraint as an architectural operation the network cannot violate, whatever it learns. A stoichiometric null-space projection cannot leak mass. A directionality gate cannot let an irreversible reaction run backward. The guarantee comes from linear algebra, not from training convergence.

The same toolkit — hard architectural constraints, elastic-weight-consolidated online adaptation, and Jacobian-norm bifurcation detection — recurs across three independent biological simulators: a multi-well organoid-monitoring system fusing biosensor data, a metabolic flux surrogate built from scratch, and a cortical-microcircuit surrogate built on connectivity internals I already maintain through my Google Summer of Code work. Related projects extend this same scientific-ML mindset — evidence and uncertainty over convenience — to autonomous molecular discovery and retrieval-grounded biomedical AI.

OrganoidTwin

Results validated

Organoid Systems · Biosensor Fusion · Computational Biology

A graph neural network that fuses multi-sensor biosensor data to monitor simulated organoid cultures in real time, flag early decline, and test whether automated intervention actually improves outcomes.

Research question
Organoid cultures can decline for mechanistically different reasons — nutrient/oxygen depletion vs. contamination-driven damage — that aren't distinguishable from any single sensor alone. Can fusing four noisy sensor streams per well through a graph model, reasoning over an entire plate rather than each well in isolation, catch decline earlier and route it to the right response?
Approach
  • Built a biologically-grounded simulation from scratch: a metabolic flux model tracking glucose, oxygen, and lactate per well, including a Warburg-shift mechanism and two mechanistically distinct decline pathways — reversible substrate limitation vs. permanent adverse-event damage.
  • Simulated realistic noisy, laggy sensor readings (pH, dissolved O₂, glucose/lactate proxy, impedance) from that hidden ground truth, so the model only ever sees what a real deployment would see.
  • A custom graph neural network — a temporal GRU encoder feeding a hand-implemented dense GATv2 attention layer, no external graph library — fusing all four sensor streams across a 24-well plate simultaneously, with multi-task heads for health-score regression, an auxiliary O₂/lactate flux prediction tied to the health head by a hard stoichiometric constraint, and a 4-way root-cause classifier (healthy / oxygen-limited / glucose-limited / adverse event).
  • MC-dropout for predictive uncertainty and a Jacobian-sensitivity bifurcation detector that flags early regime-shift warnings, calibrated from known-healthy reference periods rather than a live baseline.
  • Elastic Weight Consolidation for continual learning, letting the model periodically fine-tune on live-streamed conditions without catastrophically forgetting its original training distribution.
  • A rule-based recommendation engine driven by the learned cause classifier, benchmarked against a heuristic-only baseline.

Result / contribution

A validated closed-loop monitoring system: the cause-classifier-driven recommendation engine outperforms a heuristic-only baseline (47.9% vs. 28.7% accuracy, n=94 flagged events), the uncertainty estimate correlates meaningfully with actual error (Spearman ρ=0.64, p<0.001) though it under-covers relative to its nominal target, and the closed-loop control experiment includes a built-in negative control that behaves exactly as expected — validating the experiment design itself. All of this is demonstrated on a from-scratch synthetic simulation, not real lab data: the contribution is the system design — multi-modal sensor fusion, calibrated uncertainty, causal-attribution-driven control, and statistically rigorous experimentation with negative controls — not a biological finding.

Cell Digital Twin

Core system built

Metabolism · Computational Biology

Constraint-embedded graph neural networks for real-time metabolic flux inference.

Research question
Can a graph neural network match the biological validity of LP-solver-based flux balance analysis — exact mass conservation, correct reaction directionality — while running fast enough for real-time simulation, and while continuing to adapt as new physiological conditions are observed?
Approach
  • A 3-layer GATv2 with FiLM-based environmental conditioning as a real-time neural surrogate for cellular metabolic state.
  • Mass conservation as a hard architectural constraint: an SVD-derived stoichiometric null-space projection inside the forward pass, so every prediction is conservation-valid independent of training convergence — not a soft penalty an undertrained model could still violate.
  • Thermodynamic directionality baked into the architecture: a KEGG-annotated reversibility mask selects a sharp (β=6) vs. gentle (β=1) softplus, enforcing irreversible-reaction behavior rather than hoping the network learns it from data.
  • An online continual-learning loop with Elastic Weight Consolidation: the deployed model fine-tunes on newly observed (glucose, oxygen, enzyme, temperature) regimes streamed over WebSocket, Fisher-weighted against catastrophic forgetting.
  • A Jacobian-norm regime-transition detector — ‖∂flux/∂env‖ via autograd — flagging qualitative shifts such as aerobic respiration → Warburg-like fermentation, using an adaptively calibrated statistical threshold.

SimuLab

Active development

Molecular Design · AI for Biological Data

Agentic molecule optimization through a simulated wet lab, with tool co-evolution.

Research question
Can a scientific AI agent improve the reliability of the very tools it depends on, rather than treating them as fixed — and does that co-evolution beat a frozen-tool baseline on sample efficiency and final candidate quality?
Approach
  • An LLM-driven agent that plans and executes molecule-optimization campaigns inside a simulated wet lab, with a resource-constrained experiment budget standing in for real assay cost.
  • GNN-based predictors for binding affinity, toxicity, and solubility, each with calibrated noise and self-reported predictive uncertainty in place of a real assay.
  • An active-learning loop in which the agent's own exploration into high-uncertainty regions of chemical space triggers targeted fine-tuning of the prediction tools it just used — the tool improves because the agent used it.

HNN-Core Surrogate

Active development

Cortical Circuits · Computational Neuroscience

A constrained, continually-adapting differentiable surrogate for cortical microcircuit dynamics.

Research question
NEURON-based biophysical simulators are too slow to support large-scale parameter inference or uncertainty quantification against empirical MEG/EEG data — can the constrained-GNN methodology from Project 1 close that gap on a second, structurally different biological system?
Approach
  • A graph representation of the cortical microcircuit — cell populations/compartments as nodes, synaptic connections as edges — using the per-synapse connectivity DataFrame (source/target GID, section, segment, receptor, weight) I designed during my GSoC refactor of HNN-Core.
  • Hard biophysical constraints as architectural gates, mirroring Project 1's directionality gate: Dale's Law (a cell's outgoing synapses are all-excitatory or all-inhibitory) and non-negative conduction delays, enforced structurally rather than through a soft penalty.
  • EWC-based continual adaptation as new drive patterns or connectivity configurations are explored, reusing Project 1's EWC machinery on a different model family.
  • A Jacobian-based bifurcation detector aimed at transitions between qualitatively different oscillatory regimes — the same probe as Project 1, applied to a system where "regime change" already has direct scientific meaning.

Materials Science · Applied ML

Research question
Can a machine-learned model predict CVD-grown 2D material quality directly from growth-chamber parameters, well enough to replace trial-and-error process tuning?
Approach

Bayesian Optimization of CVD Growth Conditions

Results validated

Materials Science · Bayesian ML

Minimizing the number of experiments needed to reach a target CVD material-quality threshold.

Research question
Can Bayesian optimization reach a target CVD material-quality threshold in substantially fewer experiments than random or grid search?
Approach
  • Trained a Gaussian Process surrogate model on 20–50 initial CVD runs across 9 process variables.
  • Used a Bayesian acquisition function to select the next most-informative experiment, updating the model with each run's microscopy/Raman results.
  • Formulated a multi-objective target maximizing domain size, coverage, and Raman quality while minimizing defect density and FWHM, targeting a quality score above 90/100.

Evidence-Grounded Clinical RAG System

Core system built

Biomedical AI · Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A longitudinal clinical RAG pipeline that grounds patient-specific responses in verifiable biomedical literature and clinical guidelines.

Research question
Can a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline ground patient-specific clinical responses in verifiable biomedical literature, while reliably flagging claims it cannot support?
Approach
  • Built a hybrid retrieval pipeline (BM25 + dense embedding retrieval + cross-encoder reranking) surfacing the top 5–20 most relevant evidence sources per query, integrated with 10–30 lab measurements, 1–10 medications, and 5–20 history events per patient.
  • Implemented claim-level verification classifying each generated claim as supported, contradicted, or unsupported against retrieved evidence, to flag unreliable outputs before they reach a clinician.
